5G NR Non-Standalone (EN-DC) — dual connectivity anchored on LTE · plain-English + how-it-works + interview Q&A
Public-knowledge write-ups of 5G NSA / EN-DC features (LTE anchor + NR leg on the EPC). Concepts only, no internal specifics. Each card feeds the Interview simulator.
NN01 EN-DC / Non-Standalone Overview 5G NSA click to expand ▾
In plain English
The fast path to 5G: keep the LTE core and anchor, add a 5G leg for extra throughput. This is what "NSA" means.
How it works
In EN-DC (E-UTRA–NR Dual Connectivity) the LTE eNB is the Master Node (MeNB) anchoring control/signalling to the EPC; the gNB is the Secondary Node (SgNB) adding an NR user-plane leg. The UE is connected to both simultaneously.
Interview Q&A
Q: What is NSA / EN-DC and why did operators start there?
A: NSA (EN-DC) attaches a 5G NR secondary node to an existing LTE anchor and EPC, so the LTE cell handles mobility/signalling while NR boosts throughput. It let operators launch 5G data without first deploying a 5G core — faster and cheaper than standalone.
NN02 SgNB Addition Procedure 5G NSA click to expand ▾
In plain English
The signalling that bolts the 5G leg onto an existing LTE connection.
How it works
The LTE UE reports NR measurements (event B1); the MeNB sends SgNB Addition Request over X2-C; the SgNB admits resources and returns its NR RRC config; the MeNB forwards it to the UE in an RRCConnectionReconfiguration; the UE does RACH to the gNB and confirms — now dual-connected.

Interview Q&A
Q: Walk the SgNB addition in EN-DC.
A: The UE sends a B1 measurement report (NR above threshold) to the MeNB. MeNB → SgNB Addition Request (X2AP) with UE capabilities/QoS. SgNB admits, allocates NR resources, replies with its RRC config. MeNB relays it to the UE (RRC reconfiguration). UE performs random access to the gNB and sends reconfiguration complete → the NR leg is active.
NN03 Measurement-Based CA & EN-DC 5G NSA click to expand ▾
In plain English
Add the 5G leg (and extra carriers) only when the UE actually measures good NR — not blindly.
How it works
The eNB configures NR/inter-freq measurements; when the reported quality crosses the configured threshold (B1/A4-type events), it triggers SgNB addition or SCell activation, and removes them when quality drops — keeping links only where they help.
Interview Q&A
Q: Why gate EN-DC/SCell addition on measurements?
A: Adding an NR leg or SCell that the UE can't sustain wastes signalling and can hurt throughput/battery. Triggering on measurement events (e.g. B1 for NR) ensures the extra carrier is added only when the UE genuinely sees usable quality, and dropped when it fades.
NN04 NSA Packet Split (Split Bearer) 5G NSA click to expand ▾
In plain English
Send one data flow down both the LTE and NR legs at once, splitting/recombining at a single anchor point for more throughput.
How it works
In EN-DC the PDCP layer (at the SgNB for a typical SCG-split bearer) splits DL traffic across the LTE and NR RLC legs and reorders on receipt. Bearer types: MCG (LTE only), SCG (NR only), and split (both).
Interview Q&A
Q: What are the EN-DC bearer types and what does split bearer do?
A: MCG bearer uses only the LTE leg, SCG bearer only the NR leg, and split bearer uses both — a single PDCP entity distributes PDUs over the LTE and NR RLC paths and reorders at the receiver. Split bearer aggregates throughput but needs PDCP reordering to handle the two legs' different delays.
NN05 NSA Data Forwarding 5G NSA click to expand ▾
In plain English
Don't lose in-flight packets when the 5G secondary node changes or is released.
How it works
On SgNB change/release the source forwards buffered PDCP data to the target (or back to the MeNB) over X2-U so the flow continues; PDCP sequence numbers keep ordering and avoid duplicates.
Interview Q&A
Q: When is data forwarding needed in NSA?
A: On SgNB change or release (and at split-bearer reconfiguration), packets buffered on the NR leg would otherwise be lost. The source forwards outstanding PDCP PDUs to the new node or the master over X2-U, and PDCP sequencing preserves order and removes duplicates.
NN06 NSA + Carrier Aggregation Simultaneously 5G NSA click to expand ▾
In plain English
Run LTE carrier aggregation and the NR leg at the same time for maximum aggregate throughput.
How it works
The UE aggregates multiple LTE carriers (PCell + SCells) under the MeNB and carries NR SCG carriers under the SgNB concurrently, subject to its band-combination capability and power/processing limits.
Interview Q&A
Q: What limits how many carriers a UE can run across LTE CA + EN-DC?
A: The UE's supported band combinations and its baseband/RF and power budget. Each added carrier costs processing and (for UL) transmit power; the UE advertises the legal combinations in its capability, and the network must stay within them.
NN07 NSA with VoLTE 5G NSA click to expand ▾
In plain English
In NSA, voice stays on LTE (VoLTE) while NR just boosts data — because there's no 5G core for voice.
How it works
The LTE anchor keeps the IMS/VoLTE bearers (QCI 1/5); the NR SCG carries best-effort data. If NR power/coverage would harm the voice call, the network can drop the NR leg to protect VoLTE.
Interview Q&A
Q: How is voice handled in NSA?
A: As VoLTE on the LTE anchor — NSA has no 5G core, so voice uses the existing EPC/IMS path (QCI 1). The NR secondary node only adds data throughput; the network may release the NR leg if it threatens the voice bearer's quality or the UE's power budget.
NN08 5G Cell Change (SgNB Change) 5G NSA click to expand ▾
In plain English
Move the NR leg from one gNB/cell to another as the UE moves, without disturbing the LTE anchor.
How it works
On NR measurement events the MeNB coordinates an SgNB Change (release old + add new SgNB) over X2, with data forwarding; the LTE MCG connection stays put, so mobility of the 5G leg is transparent to the anchor.
Interview Q&A
Q: How does NR-leg mobility work in NSA without dropping the connection?
A: The LTE MeNB stays the anchor; only the secondary node changes via SgNB change (add target, release source) triggered by NR measurements, with PDCP data forwarding. Because control/mobility is anchored on LTE, the 5G leg can be swapped underneath without a full handover.
NN09 NSA & SA on the Same NR Cell 5G NSA click to expand ▾
In plain English
Let one NR cell serve both NSA (EN-DC) UEs and native SA UEs at the same time.
How it works
The gNB cell advertises support for both modes and connects to both the EPC (via the LTE anchor over X2) and the 5GC (via NG), scheduling NSA and SA UEs on shared PHY resources — easing migration from NSA to SA.
Interview Q&A
Q: Why run NSA and SA on the same NR cell?
A: It lets operators migrate gradually: legacy EN-DC handsets keep working via the LTE anchor while newer SA-capable devices use the 5G core on the same carrier — no separate NR deployment per mode. The gNB must terminate both X2 (to the eNB/EPC) and NG (to the 5GC).
NN10 4G–5G X2 Interface (EN-DC) 5G NSA click to expand ▾
In plain English
The interface that ties the LTE anchor and the 5G secondary node together in NSA.
How it works
EN-DC extends X2 with EN-DC-specific control (X2-C: SgNB add/modify/release, coordination) and X2-U user-plane forwarding for split/SCG bearers between the eNB and en-gNB.
Interview Q&A
Q: What role does X2 play in EN-DC?
A: It connects the MeNB (LTE) and the en-gNB (NR). X2-C carries the SgNB addition/modification/release and coordination signalling; X2-U carries user-plane data forwarding for SCG/split bearers. It's the backbone that makes dual connectivity work.
NN11 SA vs NSA — architecture at a glance (animated) 5G NSA click to expand ▾
In plain English
The one-picture difference: in NSA (EN-DC, Option 3) the LTE eNB is the master anchored to the 4G core (EPC), with the NR gNB bolted on as a secondary leg. In SA (Option 2) the gNB stands alone on the 5G core (5GC) — no LTE anchor. The moving dots below trace the active control/user paths.
The one-line difference
NSA: LTE eNB (master) + NR en-gNB (secondary) → EPC (4G core); interfaces S1, X2, S1-U. LTE remains the control anchor. SA: gNB alone → 5GC; interfaces N2 (gNB↔AMF), N3 (gNB↔UPF), N11 (AMF↔SMF), N4 (SMF↔UPF), N6 (UPF↔DN). No LTE anchor. It's a core-network difference, not a speed ranking.
Interview Q&A
Q: In one sentence, what changes between NSA and SA?
A: The core: NSA keeps the LTE eNB as the master anchored to the EPC (4G core) with NR as a secondary leg (EN-DC), while SA runs the gNB standalone on the 5G core (5GC) with no LTE anchor — so it's a core/anchoring difference, not a raw-speed difference.
Q: Which interfaces connect the gNB to the 5G core in SA?
A: N2 carries control (gNB↔AMF) and N3 carries user data (gNB↔UPF). Inside the core, N11 links AMF↔SMF, N4 links SMF↔UPF, and N6 connects UPF↔the data network. In NSA the equivalents are S1/X2/S1-U to the EPC.
