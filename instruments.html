EXA N9010B — your primary TX measurement instrument

Key EXA specs and settings for your work:
Freq range: 10 Hz – 26.5 GHz. DANL: −163 dBm/Hz (preamp ON). Max input: +30 dBm (+20 dBm recommended with pad). Port: TCP 5025 for SCPI. Serial: MY59070653. IP: 10.62.2.135.
For ACLR: Atten 20–30 dB, preamp OFF, RBW 100 kHz, Meas→ACLR function.
For EVM: use VSA mode or dedicated EVM measurement. Input: BOB SMA + 30 dB pad.
For RSSI check: Atten 0 dB, preamp ON, narrow span around band centre.
DANL and why preamp matters:
DANL (Displayed Average Noise Level) is the EXA's own noise floor — the minimum signal it can display. With preamp OFF and 0 dB attenuation: DANL ≈ −120 dBm/Hz. With preamp ON: DANL ≈ −130 dBm/Hz. For your sensitivity tests, the signal at the RU output is around −97.5 dBm in 10 MHz = −167 dBm/Hz spectral density. EXA preamp ON gives −130 dBm/Hz — 37 dB above the signal. Fine for direct spectrum viewing. But for ACLR on TX: signal is −17 dBm at BOB SMA after pad — preamp OFF absolutely required.
Common EXA mistakes to avoid:
Preamp ON with TX signal → input compression → ACLR reads 10–15 dB better than reality (wrong, fail in field).
Attenuation too low → ADC in EXA compresses → same wrong result.
RBW too wide → noise floor appears higher → sensitivity appears worse.
Not waiting for sweep → single sweep gives noise-contaminated ACLR → always average ≥10 sweeps.
Wrong centre frequency → measuring wrong channel → wrong ACLR offsets.

VNA phase calibration — why phase matters for beamforming

Why phase calibration is required:
Beamforming steers the antenna beam by applying precise phase shifts to each column's TX signal. If Column B's actual phase is 5° different from Column A due to cable or connector variation, the beam will point 2° off from the commanded direction. Over a 200m cell radius, a 2° beam error means the signal maximum is 7m away from where you think it is — reducing effective coverage. Phase cal measures the actual phase of each column and corrects it in the PE44820DS phase shifter register.
What the VNA measures:
S21 at the TX band centre frequency (e.g. 2140 MHz for B1). Source: VNA Port 1 → RU BOB SMA (one column at a time). Sink: VNA Port 2 → reference point. The S21 phase for each column gives: electrical path length from BOB SMA to measurement plane. Difference between columns A, B, C, D = phase mismatch to correct. Target: all four columns within ±2° of Column A. If Column C shows −6.4°, adjust PE44820DS register for Column C by +6.4°.
VNA E5071C — key settings for phase cal:
Freq range: single point or narrow span (±10 MHz) around band centre. IFBW: 1 kHz (low noise for accurate phase). Power: −10 dBm (keep RU in linear region). Cal: SOLT full 2-port, performed at measurement cable tips. Warm-up: 30 minutes before any measurement. Format: Phase (degrees). Do NOT bend cables between cal and measurement — phase shifts by 2–5° per 90° bend.
Phase in your AAU — the core reason
Your Metro AAU has 12 antenna elements (B1/B3/B41) steered by digital phase shifters (PE44820DS). Each element must transmit at a precise phase relative to the others. The beam points in the direction where the phases add constructively. A 5° phase error on one element degrades the beam pattern — the main lobe shifts, the gain drops, and side lobes increase. This is why TX_CAL (transmit calibration) exists and why the VNA is your primary tool for verifying it.

VNA2 VNA calibration — SOLT/SOLR, why it matters, what errors it removes Intermediate click to expand▾
PLAIN ENGLISH FIRST VNA calibration in one sentence: Before you can trust any VNA measurement, you must remove the errors introduced by the cables and connectors between the VNA and your device. SOLT calibration (Short-Open-Load-Through) does this in 12 steps. Skip it and your S21 phase measurement includes the cable electrical length — meaningless for comparing column-to-column phase.

VNA calibration — SOLT, error models, reference plane

What SOLT calibration removes:
SOLT (Short-Open-Load-Through) calibration corrects 12 systematic errors: directivity, source match, reflection tracking, transmission tracking, load match, and isolation — for both ports. After calibration, the VNA measurement reference plane moves to the tips of the calibration standard, removing cable and connector effects. Skipping cal means your S21 phase includes the electrical length of the measurement cables — meaningless for per-column phase comparison.
12-step SOLT procedure:
1. Warm up VNA 30 min. 2. Set frequency range (e.g. 2100–2180 MHz for B1). 3. Set IFBW 1 kHz. 4. Port 1: connect SHORT, press Measure. 5. Port 1: connect OPEN, press Measure. 6. Port 1: connect LOAD (50Ω), press Measure. 7. Port 2: connect SHORT. 8. Port 2: connect OPEN. 9. Port 2: connect LOAD. 10. Connect THROUGH between ports. 11. Press Apply Cal. 12. Verify: S11 of LOAD should be < −40 dB. Then connect DUT without moving cables.
What invalidates your calibration:
Bending cables after cal, changing adapters, temperature change > 10°C, loosening any connector. If in doubt: re-cal. A 5-minute re-cal is always cheaper than a wrong measurement leading to a wrong register correction.
Why you MUST calibrate before any phase measurement
The VNA measures phase at its test ports. But everything between the VNA port and your DUT (cables, adaptors, connectors) adds phase. Without calibration, you are measuring the phase of your test setup, not the DUT. A 30 cm test cable at 2 GHz adds ~72° of phase. After SOLT calibration, the reference plane moves to the cal kit connector tips — the cable phase is mathematically removed.
SOLT calibration — Short, Open, Load, Thru
SOLT removes 12 systematic error terms: Port 1: Directivity, Source Match, Reflection Tracking Port 2: Directivity, Source Match, Reflection Tracking Transmission: Forward/Reverse Tracking, Forward/Reverse Isolation Procedure: SHORT → Γ = −1 (180° reflection) — known reflection standard OPEN → Γ = +1 (0° reflection) — known reflection standard LOAD → Γ = 0 (50Ω match) — sets the reference impedance THRU → direct connection P1→P2 — sets transmission reference

VNA3 Cable electrical length & phase matching — step-by-step for your SF141 cables Intermediate click to expand▾

Cable electrical length — phase matching, propagation delay

Electrical length vs physical length:
Electromagnetic waves travel slower in coax than in free space. Velocity factor (VF) = speed in cable / speed of light. RG316 (your lab cables): VF = 0.695. A 1m RG316 cable has electrical length = 1 / (VF × c) = 1 / (0.695 × 3e8) = 4.78 ns propagation delay = 4.78 ns × 2140 MHz × 360° = 3680° electrical length. This is why cable length directly determines S21 phase — and why cables to each column must be matched in electrical length (not just physical length).
Phase matching cables for beamforming:
For accurate beamforming, the cable from each column's BOB SMA to the measurement point must be phase-matched. Use VNA to measure S21 phase of each cable at band centre frequency. Target: all cables within ±2° of each other. If one cable is 5° longer, the column will appear 5° phase-shifted — exactly the error you are trying to measure in the RU. Use matched-pair cables (same manufacturer, same batch, same length) or trim electrical length using phase extender adapters.
Port extension — moving the reference plane after cal:
If you cal at the cable tips but then add an adapter, VNA port extension moves the reference plane electrically. Measure the adapter electrical length separately. Enter as port extension delay (ps) in VNA setup. This avoids a full re-cal whenever adapters change.
Why cable phase matching matters
In your test setup, multiple cables connect the FHGW BOB SMA ports to the EXA/EXM. For multi-port measurements (e.g. comparing TX power across 4 antenna elements simultaneously using a switch matrix), each cable must have the same electrical length — otherwise you are adding cable-to-cable phase differences to your measurement. For beamforming calibration, cable phase differences directly appear as beam pointing errors.
Electrical length vs physical length
Electrical length (degrees) = 360 × f × L × √εr / c Where: f = frequency (Hz) L = physical length (metres) εr = relative permittivity of the cable dielectric c = 3×10⁸ m/s For SF141 semi-rigid cable (εr ≈ 2.1, velocity factor VF ≈ 0.695): At 2140 MHz, 1 metre of SF141: Phase = 360 × 2140e6 × 1 × √2.1 / 3e8 = 360 × 2140e6 × 1.449 / 3e8 = 360 × 10.33 = 3720° = 10.33 full wavelengths So 1 mm difference in SF141 cable length at 2140 MHz = 3.72° phase difference For beamforming: target match within ±2° = within ±0.54 mm physical length!

Measurement procedure per column:
1. SOLT cal at cable tips. 2. Connect Port 1 to Column A BOB SMA. 3. Connect Port 2 to reference point (or use a known reference cable). 4. Measure S21 at band centre. 5. Record magnitude and phase. 6. Repeat for columns B, C, D. 7. Compute Δφ = each column phase − Column A phase. 8. Δφ > ±2° → update PE44820DS register for that column by −Δφ (opposite sign correction).
Real B1 example (unit 26050509):
Col A: −125.4° (ref). Col B: −127.1° → Δ = −1.7° (within ±2° ✓). Col C: −131.8° → Δ = −6.4° (FAIL) → adjust PE44820DS by +6.4°. Col D: −126.9° → Δ = −1.5° (within ±2° ✓). After correction: re-measure Col C → should show ≈ −125°. If not: PE44820DS register may have limited resolution (e.g. 5.625° steps) — round to nearest step.
When phase error is larger than ±5°:
Δφ > 5° usually means: cable swap (wrong cable connected to that column), connector not fully mated (1–2 turns left to tighten), or physical damage to the RF trace/connector on the BOB. Inspect physically before adjusting register — a register correction of 8° when the actual problem is a loose connector will appear to pass VNA check but drift back when temperature changes.

Each of the 8 or 12 TX paths in your AAU (from FHGW DAC output to RFFE antenna port) introduces a different phase shift. The OB_CAL (observation calibration) corrects these in firmware — but first you need to MEASURE them to know how much correction is needed.

Beamforming angle calibration — phase to beam angle mapping

Beam angle formula and your numbers:
θ = arcsin(λ × Δφ_rad / (2π × d)). For B1 at 2140 MHz: λ = c/f = 3e8/2140e6 = 0.140 m. d = λ/2 = 0.070 m. Δφ = 20° = 0.349 rad → θ = arcsin(0.140 × 0.349 / (2π × 0.070)) = arcsin(0.111) = 6.4°. This means: if each consecutive column is programmed 20° more phase shift than the previous, the beam points 6.4° off boresight.
Per-band beam angle differences:
Different bands have different wavelengths and different element spacings, so the same phase step gives a different beam angle per band. B1 (2140 MHz): 20° Δφ = 6.4° beam angle. B3 (1843 MHz): 20° Δφ = 5.5°. B8 (942 MHz): 20° Δφ = 2.8°. B28 (758 MHz): 20° Δφ = 2.2°. This is why beam calibration is done per band, not once for all bands. The PE44820DS register value for a given beam direction differs by band.
Verifying beam steering with OTA measurement:
After phase cal, verify beam steering optionally using a reference antenna on a tripod at known angle from the AAU. Measure receive power at multiple angles while sweeping phase commands. Maximum received power angle should match commanded angle within ±1°. If not: re-run VNA phase cal, check PE44820DS register writes were accepted, verify mutual coupling between columns is within design spec.
From phase difference to beam angle
For a uniform linear array with element spacing d: Beam angle θ = arcsin(λ × ΔΦ / (2π × d)) Where: λ = wavelength = c/f ΔΦ = progressive phase shift between adjacent elements (radians) d = element spacing (typically λ/2 for maximum scan range) For λ/2 spacing: d = λ/2 → θ = arcsin(ΔΦ/π) ΔΦ = 0° → θ = 0° (broadside, beam perpendicular to array) ΔΦ = 30° → θ = arcsin(30/180) = 9.6° scan ΔΦ = 60° → θ = 19.5° scan ΔΦ = 90° → θ = 30° scan ΔΦ = 180° → θ = 90° (endfire — maximum scan, impractical) Your B1/B3 4-column array (12 elements): Scan range: typically ±60° in azimuth Each 1° phase step → ~0.3° beam shift (at broadside)
Verifying the phase shifter (PE44820DS) with VNA
The PE44820DS is a digitally-controlled phase shifter. It has 6 or 8 control bits, giving 64 or 256 discrete phase states. Each state should produce a predictable, repeatable phase shift. The VNA verifies: (1) each state produces the correct phase, (2) insertion loss is consistent across states, (3) there are no stuck bits.

Practical VNA tips — common mistakes, cable handling

Six most common VNA mistakes:
1. Not warming up — LO drifts 5–10° in first 15 min. Always wait 30 min.
2. Wrong torque — SMA spec is 0.9 N·m. Over-torque destroys center pin. Always use torque wrench.
3. Cal at wrong plane — cal WITH measurement cables attached, not before.
4. Skipping isolation step — SOLT needs isolation between ports for accurate S21 at high loss.
5. Wrong IFBW — use 1 kHz for phase cal (slow but accurate), not 1 MHz (fast but noisy).
6. Moving cables after cal — phase shifts 2–5° per cable flex.
Connector inspection before every connection:
Look for: bent center pin (replace immediately), scratches on mating face (replace), debris in connector (clean with IPA and wooden toothpick — never metal). After cleaning, inspect with loupe. A bent center pin costs 2 minutes to catch and replace. A bent pin that makes contact costs a 2-day measurement mystery.
Port extension vs full recal — when to use which:
Full recal: whenever you change cable, add/remove adapter, or temperature changes > 10°C. Port extension: when you want to move reference plane by a fixed electrical delay (e.g. accounting for a short adapter). Port extension does not correct impedance mismatch — it only shifts phase. Never use port extension as a substitute for proper recal when making precision measurements.

What to do when correction does not take effect:
PE44820DS is an SPI-controlled digital phase shifter. Write via CLI: pe44820_write col_C 0x2A (register value depends on step size). Verify readback matches. Phase is frequency-dependent. A cable that is 0° at 2100 MHz may be +3° at 2180 MHz. Phase cal at band centre (2140 MHz for B1) gives the best average across the band. For wideband beamforming, some systems do phase cal at 3 frequencies (low edge, centre, high edge) and interpolate. Your RU uses single-frequency cal — adequate for 80 MHz B1 bandwidth where phase variation is small.

The 4 red cables in your diagram are NOT connected simultaneously to 4 EXM ports — the EXM has one primary RF input (RFIO). The cables go to a Mini-Circuits RF switch (HTTP-controlled, IP-addressable). RuAuto sends a switch command, then measures on EXM, then switches to the next port. This is why RCT takes several minutes — it cycles through all port/band combinations sequentially.
